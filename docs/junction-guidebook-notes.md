# Junction 2026 participant guidebook: what matters for us

Source: official Participant Guide on the Junction platform, pasted by Oliver 2026-11-13 (the page is login-only). Wi-Fi credentials are in the guide and deliberately not copied here.

## Judging (this replaces the Gavel assumptions in `demo-research.md`)

- Evaluation criteria, three only:
  - **Innovation**: new or different compared to the already presented ones.
  - **Impact**: significant impact on society; what share of the people with the problem get it solved.
  - **Implementation**: realistic, viable, feasible "from all perspectives".
- Detection quality is not a Junction criterion. It is part of Hostinger's own challenge criteria only. Hostinger judges its own challenge winner.
- Live peer review Sun 10:30 to 13:00 at tables. Each participant reviews the projects assigned to them; a team can press "not present". Teams that skip peer review are NOT eligible for the main prize.
- Top 5 by peer review pitch on stage: 3 minutes, mics cut at 3:00, then 3 minutes of questions from a panel of 7 judges. Demo from your own computer on stage, connection checked beforehand. No time to practise after review ends.
- Challenge winners are announced 15:10, the Junction winner 15:50.

## Submission

- Pre-submission (challenge pick + draft) Fri 23:59. Final Sun 10:00, no late submissions, project must be set to FINAL, drafts are not reviewed.
- Required: name, punchline, description, challenge, technologies, **video pitch max 2 minutes ("very strict")**, demo and source links, source publicity, pitch deck PDF, team phone numbers, Discord names.
- All submissions are licensed MIT. Organizers must be able to read the source.
- The guide says "You are allowed to submit a project for multiple challenges" (Tim found the form's main-challenge picker is single-select, tech challenges multi).
- Team size 1 to 4.

## Rules that bite us

- No code written before the event. All code outside open-source libraries must be written at the event. Using code written specifically for the idea in advance, **or working on a new project that is somehow related to an existing company, means disqualification.**
- Prize pool 100 000 EUR, main prize 10 000 EUR.

## Schedule points

- Fri 18:00 opening, 19:30 hacking starts, 23:59 pre-submission.
- Sat 12:00 submissions open, 13:00 to 13:45 Hostinger keynote (main stage), 13:30 Modal workshop, 17:30 Context workshop.
- Sun 10:00 final submission, 10:30 to 13:00 peer review, 14:00 ceremony, 14:15 top 5 pitching.

## What this changes

1. The audience for the main prize is other hackers at a table, plus 7 judges for the top 5. Both read Innovation, Impact, Implementation, not precision and FPR. A bot guard has to be pitched as a problem for real people (site owners and visitors wrongly blocked), with the numbers as proof underneath.
2. The 2-minute video and the 3-minute stage pitch are both hard limits. Plan one 2-minute story that works for both.
3. The table demo repeats many times in 2.5 hours for different reviewers. It must be resettable in seconds and survive venue Wi-Fi.
4. Dev tools score worst on Impact for non-technical reviewers, as Felix noted. The impact story is the open design problem.
