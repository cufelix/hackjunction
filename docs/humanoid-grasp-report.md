# Grokbot "Research" report 2: fixing the triangular-prism grasp

Condensed from the bot's reply, 2026-11-14 ~01:30. Bot claims are UNVERIFIED unless marked **checked** (confirmed by me in the starter repo at commit `36daf5b`). "Own calc" = the bot's arithmetic on the repo STL/config, a hypothesis, not a published result. Nothing built.

## Bottom line (bot)
Turn the wrist so the thumb and finger pinch along an **altitude** of the triangle (one corner against the opposite flat face), never across two faces. Altitudes repeat every 60 deg, so the wrist turns at most +-30 deg. Get the angle from `cv2.minEnclosingTriangle`, because `minAreaRect` gives the wrong yaw on triangles. Before building, run two one-hour sim checks: twisting friction and finger PD gains.

## Findings, most applicable first
1. **Pinch direction decides whether it holds.** Two contacts hold an object only if the line between them stays inside both friction cones (Nguyen 1988). Corner vs opposite face needs about no friction at perfect yaw and squeezing pushes the prism back into place (altitude is its narrowest width, Goldberg 1993). Face vs face needs mu >= tan 30 deg = 0.58 even at perfect yaw and squeezing pushes it out like a wedge (own calc). Caveat: Goldberg assumes frictionless parallel jaws, so this explains the spin qualitatively, not a guarantee for Dex3 pads.
2. **Yaw error means almost no squeeze (own calc).** Fillets make the corner-to-face width 42.5 mm, not 45. At 10 deg off an altitude the chord is 38.6 mm, at 30 deg 34.6 mm. **Checked**: the baseline "closed" pose leaves a 3.6 cm gap = 0.9 cm squeeze on a 4.5 cm object (`baselines/classical/config.yaml:104-105`). With position control, grip force scales with the leftover gap, so an object 15 deg or more off barely gets squeezed. With a fixed hand yaw, about 2/3 of prisms land 10 deg or more off an altitude (own calc).
3. **The baseline yaw is wrong for triangles.** `minAreaRect` always aligns with a hull edge and all three edges give near-equal rectangles; the angle is then folded to +-45 deg. Bot's synthetic test with 1 mm noise: minAreaRect errors up to about 30 deg, `cv2.minEnclosingTriangle` on top-face depth points within about 4.5 deg. Corner vs face is easy from above (corners 27.5 mm from centre, faces 15 mm). Shape test: hull area / minAreaRect area is about 0.5 triangle, 1 cube, 0.785 cylinder (own calc). Indirect evidence: symmetry-aware angle output lifted top-down grasp success 53.6% to 70.0% (arXiv 2410.04826, parallel grippers in clutter).
4. **Sim fingers may be far stiffer than real.** **Checked**: real hand is commanded at kp 1.5, kd 0.2 (`interface/backends.py:202`). Bot says the upstream sim uses stiffness 100, damping 10, effort 300 (`unitree_sim_isaaclab/robots/unitree.py`, NOT checked, and the units may differ). Organisers say friction and PD gains are not fitted to the real robot (also in the sim README "Still open", N1). Unitree publishes no grip force, only "500 g payload on a 5 cm round object".
5. **The sim has no twisting friction.** PhysX applies no torsional friction unless a contact patch radius is set. **Checked**: `sim/aalto_task/scene_cfg.py` sets friction, restitution and contact offsets but no torsional/patch setting. Consistent with "prisms spin in the pinch". Test 2-5 mm (bot's guess).
6. **Sim friction is high.** **Checked**: objects use static 1.2 / dynamic 1.0 with friction combine mode "max" (`scene_cfg.py:41-42, 228`), so every object contact, including the counter, gets at least 1.2. **Checked**: DR varies friction only x0.75-1.1 and mass x0.7-1.5 around a 50 g nominal (`sim/dr/train.yaml:20-21`). Bot's numbers for real PLA: PLA-on-PLA 0.12-0.38 (abstract only), 0.33-0.82 in impact tests; PLA-on-rubber not found. If real fingertip mu is under about 0.6, face-vs-face pinches fail at every yaw. DeXtreme randomised object friction x0.3-0.9, gains x0.3-3, restitution 0-0.4 (arXiv 2210.13702).
7. **Dex3 hardware.** One real G1's thumb rotation joint ignored commands leaving a gap of about 5 cm, fixed by a driver reflash (xr_teleoperate #229): check `hand_q[0]` tracking on day 1 and aim with wrist yaw. Joint ranges as in the Unitree page. Pad material not published. The policy sees only `hand_q`, no velocity or torque. Self-collision is off in the sim hand.
8. **Failed-grasp detection without touch.** "Fully closed means empty": the BEHAVIOR 2025 winner reopened and "approximately doubled" success on selected tasks (arXiv 2512.06951, thin evidence: 13 tasks x 3 episodes). Finger encoders alone detected failed grasp-and-lift 96.97% of the time on ARMAR-DE (165 runs, KIT). Compare commanded vs measured hand angle: low real kp means a blocked finger sits short of its target while an empty hand reaches it. A camera check after the lift can miss small objects (QT-Opt). The sim never bounces (restitution 0) but real PLA bounces at 0.32-0.40 (MDPI), so release lower and nearer the bin centre than the baseline's rim + 4 cm.
9. **Published 3-finger results barely transfer.** No success rates found for small triangular prisms with Barrett, Allegro, Inspire, Robotiq or Dex3 (UNVERIFIED that none exist). Thumb-index-middle grasp 93.3% on household objects (Bai 2021). Learned detectors (Dex-Net 2.0, GPD, Contact-GraspNet) are all parallel-jaw.

## Bot's 5 options (effort in hours, bot's estimate)
1. Sim fixes first: twisting-friction patch radius, finger stiffness/effort toward real, rerun the oracle to see how much of the 16% loss was a sim artefact. 2-4 h.
2. Altitude-aligned pinch: `minEnclosingTriangle` yaw, wrist to the nearest altitude (<= 30 deg), hand target 6-9 mm past the 42.5 mm width, contacts at 22.5 mm height. The core fix. 4-6 h.
3. Option 2 plus recovery ("fully closed means empty", camera check after lift, one retry, lower central release). +3-4 h.
4. Tripod: index and middle on the two corners of the near face, thumb on the far corner. Untested, needs fingertip pads spanning 48.3 mm, check the URDF. 6-10 h.
5. Learned correction on scripted randomised rollouts, yaw encoded as sin 6theta / cos 6theta (GG-CNN idea). The originality angle. 10-16 h.
